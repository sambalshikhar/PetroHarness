# Mount Messenger, well tuhua-7 — porosity, water saturation, and hydrocarbon potential

**Bottom line:** The Mount Messenger in tuhua-7 (590.55–873.8616 m) is a shale-prone, low-porosity interval with uninvaded-zone water saturation averaging 0.77 in the six lithology-defined zones and 0.57 in 33 unzoned samples. No part of the zoned formation carries net-pay or crossover evidence; the only pay-flag signal sits in 5 of those 33 unzoned samples and is weak and data-poor. No gas or invaded-hydrocarbon indicator is present anywhere.

---

## Point 1 — Porosity and water saturation of the Mount Messenger, and is any of it hydrocarbon-bearing?

**Where:** The six zones (590.55–873.8616 m, 1860 samples at 0.1524 m) plus 33 unzoned samples.

**How much / how:**

| Curve | Z1 (no lithology) | Z2 | Z3 | Z4 | Z5 | Z6 | Zones pooled | Unzoned (33 samples) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SW** (uninvaded, v/v) | none (92 samples) | 0.7008 | 0.7785 | 0.7501 | 0.8150 | 0.7050 | 0.7701 | **0.5737** |
| **PHIE** (v/v) | none | 0.1116 | 0.0803 | 0.1112 | 0.0668 | 0.0997 | 0.0846 | 0.2114 |
| **PHIT** (v/v) | none | 0.3098 | 0.2760 | 0.2786 | 0.2665 | 0.2983 | 0.2791 | 0.3297 |
| **VWCL** (v/v) | none | 0.8520 | 0.8534 | 0.7549 | 0.8749 | 0.8118 | 0.8463 | 0.4978 |
| **PERM** (mD, geometric mean) | none | 29.5 | 7.6 | 22.6 | 3.5 | 16.7 | 10.5 | 278.3 |
| **BVW** (v/v) | none | 0.0709 | 0.0590 | 0.0805 | 0.0514 | 0.0634 | 0.0609 | 0.1139 |

- SW range in the interval is 0.388–1.0 v/v; the value 1.0 is a model limit/default in 6% of zoned samples and should not be read as a measured water saturation.
- Z1 (590.55–604.4184 m) contributes no SW, PHIE, PHIT, or VCL values (92 samples with no lithology data), so its porosity and saturation cannot be stated from these logs.

**Is any of it hydrocarbon-bearing?** No part of the zoned formation is flagged as hydrocarbon-bearing pay. NET_PAY is 0/140, 0/1191, 0/124, 0/186, and 0/94 in Z2–Z6 — none of the 1735 zoned samples qualify. The hydrocarbon analyzers classify every zoned sample as NO_INDICATION (geophysical) or MARGINAL (integrated), with 46% of zoned thickness rated NON_RESERVOIR. The only NET_PAY flags (5 samples, 0.7616 m) occur in the 33 unzoned samples at 626.0592–626.6688 m.

**Why:** SW and PHIE are model outputs of the saturation and porosity steps (archie+indonesia model; component-based porosity). No lithology log was available for Z1, and VCL is high across the whole interval, so rock-quality flags fail. The 33 unzoned samples are the only source of both the lower SW mean and the sole pay flags.

## Point 2 — Effective and total porosity zone by zone (given VCL ~0.85)

**Effective vs. total porosity:** PHIE is consistently 0.066–0.112 v/v in the zones, while PHIT is 0.267–0.310 v/v. The gap (~0.16–0.25 v/v) tracks the shale content (VCL 0.75–0.875, pooled 0.846) via the component model `PHIE = PHIT − VCL × φ_cl` with φ_cl = 0.219. The relation PHIE < PHIT holds everywhere and is strongest in the shale-rich zones.

| Zone | Top (m) | Base (m) | VCL mean | PHIT mean | PHIE mean | PERM mean (mD) | Thickness (m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | 590.55 | 604.4184 | none | none | none | none | 13.87 |
| Z2 | 604.5708 | 625.7544 | 0.852 | 0.310 | 0.112 | 29.5 | 21.18 |
| Z3 | 626.9736 | 808.3296 | 0.853 | 0.276 | 0.080 | 7.6 | 181.36 |
| Z4 | 810.6156 | 829.3608 | 0.755 | 0.279 | 0.111 | 22.6 | 18.75 |
| Z5 | 829.6656 | 857.8596 | 0.875 | 0.267 | 0.067 | 3.5 | 28.19 |
| Z6 | 859.6884 | 873.8616 | 0.812 | 0.298 | 0.100 | 16.7 | 14.17 |

- **Weak, partial signal:** Z2 and Z4 show the highest PHIE (~0.11) and moderately higher PERM (~22–30 mD); Z5 shows the lowest PHIE (~0.067) and lowest PERM (~3.5 mD). These differences are real in the means but are thin-zone samples within a dominantly shale-prone formation.
- The 33 unzoned samples read higher (PHIE 0.211, PHIT 0.330) but n = 33, no lithology classification, and possibly bad-hole-affected; they should not be extrapolated as reservoir.

**Limits:** PHIE/PHIT are model outputs with PHIS from Wyllie time-average and PHIT from an adaptive neutron–density route (several fallback routes used in the well). The lithology group was inferred from mineral volumes because no lithology curves existed.

## Point 3 — Where is water saturation low relative to SW < 0.7, and how does SW vary between zoned and unzoned samples?

**Where SW is low:** No zone mean drops below the 0.7 pay cutoff. The closest zone means are Z2 (0.7008) and Z6 (0.7050), marginally above it. Below 0.7, SW exists only as scattered points: the petrophysical analyzer’s **LOW_SW** class (criterion SW < 0.50) covers 9 thin runs totaling **6.51 m** (2.35% of zoned thickness) in Z2, Z3, Z4, and Z6, representing **2.4% of the zone samples**. The stricter SW < 0.7 pay cutoff meets those same low points plus a few in the 0.50–0.70 band.

**Zoned vs. unzoned variation:**

| Group | n | Mean SW | Relation to 0.7 cutoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zoned zones pooled | 1735 of 1827 | **0.7701** | 73% of samples above 0.7 |
| Whole interval (includes unzoned) | 1768 of 1860 | 0.7664 | mostly above 0.7 |
| Unzoned samples | 33 | **0.5737** | 15.2% below 0.7 (the 5 NET_PAY samples) |

The unzoned samples average 0.196 lower than the zoned sample mean, but with n = 33 and no lithology context, this is a partial signal that should be reported as weak and placed rather than interpreted as a reservoir trend. The deeper/central Z3 (626.97–808.33 m, 181 m thick) is the largest shale-prone unit and carries the highest mean SW (0.7785).

## Point 4 — Hydrocarbon-bearing pay: NET_PAY, MHI/SXO, and crossover flags

**NET_PAY:** The flag requires (i) NET_ROCK: VCL < 0.30, (ii) NET_RES: PHIE > 0.08, (iii) SW < 0.70. Because VCL averages 0.75–0.87 everywhere, NET_ROCK is set in only **6 samples (0.9144 m)**, NET_RES in **5 samples (0.762 m)**, and NET_PAY in the same **5 samples (0.762 m)**. All five pay samples are outside the six zones (626.0592–626.6688 m), where VCL is lower (unzoned mean 0.498) but lithology is not classified. **0.0% of zoned samples qualify as pay.**

**Independent support from MHI/SXO:** There is none. SXO is not a flushed-zone resistivity reading; it is the quicklook transform `SW^(1/5)` (used because shallow resistivity RXO/RSHA/RESS had no usable finite samples), and MHI is the analyzer’s fallback `SW/SXO ≈ SW^0.8`. The movability analyzer therefore reports **MINIMAL_MOVEABLE_HC** (MHI ≥ 0.9, background class) in 14.4% of zone samples, **LOW_MOVEABLE_HC** (0.7–0.9) in 70.8%, **MODERATE_MOVEABLE_HC** (0.5–0.7) in 9.6%, and only **0.2% HIGH_MOVEABLE_HC** (a single 0.605 m run in Z2 at 625.30–625.75 m). These bands reflect wet, low-invasion conditions derived from SW alone, not independently confirmed hydrocarbon movability.

**Crossover flags:** CROSSOVER_FLAG (PHID − PHIN > 0.05 in clean, porous, good-hole rock, i.e., VCL < 0.35, PHIE > 0.05, BAD_HOLE < 0.5) is **0% in every zone and in the unzoned samples**. The geophysical gas analyzer returns NO_INDICATION across all 278 m of zoned thickness. Gas-correction/ND-separation analysis likewise finds NO_GAS_EFFECT (95% of zoned thickness) and only NO_GAS_INDICATORS in the data-less Z1.

**Conclusion:** No part of the Mount Messenger in tuhua-7 carries pay or gas-indicator support within the zones. The unzoned pay flag is the only positive, and it lacks lithology, gas, and crossover corroboration.

## Point 5 — Pay signal outside the zoned intervals and its trustworthiness

**Size of the out-of-zone signal:** The entire NET_PAY signal in the well is **0.7616 m across 5 of 33 unzoned samples** (626.0592–626.6688 m), i.e., 0.3% of the gross interval. The unzoned SW mean is 0.5737 versus 0.7664 for the whole interval including those samples.

**Can it be trusted given bad-hole conditions?**

- **Bad-hole extent:** 200 of 1860 interval samples are flagged BAD_HOLE (washout or |DRHO| > 0.15 g/cm³). Inside the zones this is 28.4952 m (10.3% of zoned thickness), with Z6 worst at 57.4% of its samples affected and Z2 next at 20.7%. In the unzoned 33 samples, BAD_HOLE runs total 1.8278 m in 3 runs, including one at **626.8212 m** — immediately adjacent to the pay run at 626.0592–626.6688 m. So the pay-bearing depth sits in a bad-hole-prone stretch of the unzoned interval, and the same problem likely affects the SW/PHIE values there.
- **Missing corroboration:** No lithology classification for Z1; the unzoned samples are not classified by the hydrocarbon analyzers. NET_PAY there is the *only* flag, and the MHI/SXO support is circular (quicklook transforms of SW), adding no invasion evidence. No shallow resistivity or flushed-zone curves (RXO, RSHA, RMED absent or non-finite) were available to test invasion or resistivity separation.
- **Water, not oil:** Even where SW is lowest, BVW averages 0.051–0.081 v/v in the zones and 0.114 v/v in the unzoned samples, consistent with a water-dominated fluid system.

**Reading:** The 0.7616 m of pay flag is a weak, isolated, low-sample-count artifact of the pay cutoff applied to unzoned, lithology-unknown, bad-hole-affected samples. It is not trusted as pay; the formation as a whole is shale-prone, water-saturated, and non-petroleum-bearing in every robust sense.

## Evidence and limitations the answer rests on

**Curves that are model outputs, fallbacks, or held at limits:**
- SW, PHIE, PHIT, BVW, VCL, PERM, CROSSOVER_FLAG, NET_PAY, NET_RES, NET_ROCK, BAD_HOLE, and the analyzer MHI/ND_SEPARATION are all model outputs or workflow outputs, not measurements.
- SXO is a fallback quicklook `SW^(1/5)`; MHI is an analyzer’s own computation (`SW^0.8`) because the workflow wrote no MHI. Neither is a flushed-zone resistivity reading.
- Invasion ratio (`RXO/Rt`) has values in only 1768 of 1860 samples; the medium/deep proxy was not used, and shallow samples were dropped as invalid or RT < 0 or ratio > 100. No invasion evidence survives.
- PHIT used adaptive neutron–density fallback routes (density_from_missing_phin, density_from_gas_crossover, weighted_nd, neutron_from_missing_phid). Gas crossover found no significant gas effect.

**Logs missing from this well that the question needs:**
- No lithology curves (DISO, or lithology log) were present; Z1 is labeled NO_LITHOLOGY_DATA and lithology was inferred from mineral volumes.
- Shallow/flushed resistivity (RXO, RSHA, RMED) absent or unusable, so flushed-zone water saturation and resistivity-based pay are unverifiable.
- No BHT temperature anchor; temperature uses a 30 °C/km fallback gradient (formation temperature 109–114.3 °F in this interval).
- DTS sonic present as DTC, but no lithology or sonic-quality curves for crossover validation.

**Workflow sections (computed record) and my grading:**
- `hydrocarbon.petrophysical_hydrocarbon_indicators`: LOW_SW (SW < 0.50) covers 6.51 m / 2.4% of zone samples; everything else NO_INDICATION.
- `hydrocarbon.geophysical_hydrocarbon_indicators`: NO_INDICATION 100% of zoned thickness; CROSSOVER_FLAG set in 0 samples.
- `hydrocarbon.integrated_hydrocarbon_potential`: MARGINAL 54% of zoned thickness, NON_RESERVOIR 46% (Z5 dominates NON_RESERVOIR).
- `hydrocarbon.reservoir_quality_for_hydrocarbons`: GOOD_RESERVOIR 22.7%, FAIR 21.7%, EXCELLENT 4.64%, NON_RESERVOIR 46%, UNDETERMINED 5% (Z1).
- `numerical.net_pay_flag_analysis`: NON_PAY 100% of zone samples.
- `saturation.hydrocarbon_movability_invasion_characteristics`: dominated by LOW/MINIMAL moveability because MHI ≈ SW^0.8.

**Where the workflow’s classifications and my statistics disagree:** The reservoir-quality analyzer promotes 12.9 m to EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR (PERM > 10 mD, PHIE > 0.15) on a few runs, but because VCL is ~0.85, NET_PAY still fails everywhere; excellent permeability coexists with shale lithology, so I do not grade the formation as reservoir-capable. The LOW_SW petrophysical class (2.4% of zone samples) is real but does not satisfy NET_RES due to VCL > 0.30, so it is reported as scattered low saturation, not pay.

**What bears on the question and what is extra:** I have restricted this to SW, PHIE/PHIT, VCL, NET_PAY, CROSSOVER_FLAG, MHI/SXO, and BAD_HOLE, because they directly answer the question. Resistivity normalization (RDEP), temperature, permeability beyond grade, and geomechanical curves are mentioned only where they affect saturation or data quality.
