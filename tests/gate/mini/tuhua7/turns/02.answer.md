# Bottom line

**Z2 (604.5708–625.7544 m) ranks highest on the mean-based reservoir-quality indices — PERM mean 29.52 mD / median 9.26 mD / p90 117.456 mD, FZI 2.4693 µm, RQI mean 0.3588 µm — and it sits on the best effective porosity of the zoned rock (PHIE mean 0.1116 v/v).** However, the ranking is not decisive: Z4 (810.6156–829.3608 m) edges Z2 on geometric-mean permeability (9.2675 vs 3.8524 mD), and Z6 (859.6884–873.8616 m) edges Z2 on FZI and RQI **medians** (FZI median 2.4913 vs 2.467 µm; RQI median 0.3105 vs 0.2987 µm). All six zones are logged as SHALE-PRONE (VCL BAND), 100% SHALE_DOMINATED on the permeability variation control, and NET_PAY is set on 0% of zoned samples — so the quality gap is porosity-driven within a shale-prone interval, not a lithological change.

## Point 2 — Which zone tops PERM, RQI and FZI?

- **PERM:** Z2 tops the mean (29.5247 mD), the median (9.2649 mD) and the p90 (117.456 mD). Z4 tops the geometric mean (9.2675 mD) and the p10 (3.3302 mD). Z5 is lowest on every PERM statistic (mean 3.4936, geo 0.6267, median 1.5159).
- **FZI:** Z2 is highest by mean (2.4693 µm), ahead of Z4 (2.4513 µm; quantifies the gap as 0.018), then Z6 (2.3626 µm), Z3 (2.1418 µm), Z5 (1.9888 µm). Z2's class view in the result is MACRO_PORES with 89.3% of samples in that class.
- **RQI:** Z2 is highest by mean (0.3588 µm), then Z4 (0.324 µm), then Z6 (0.3054 µm). By median, however, Z6 (0.3105 µm) exceeds Z2 (0.2987 µm) and Z4 (0.2694 µm).

**No zone tops all three.** Z2 tops PERM mean/median, FZI mean, and RQI mean; Z4 tops PERM geo-mean and p10; Z6 tops PERM median (8.5266 vs Z4's 7.4186) and RQI/FZI medians.

## Point 3 — Do the three indices rank the zones the same way?

**No. The indices do not fully agree, and the ranking flips on the statistic chosen.** lists the highest zone by each PERM statistic: median Z2, geo-mean Z4, mean Z2, p10 Z4, p90 Z2 — so on permeability alone, Z2 and Z4 trade places. FZI is consistent on its mean (Z2 > Z4 > Z6). RQI splits exactly where PERM splits: on means Z2 > Z4 > Z6, on medians Z6 > Z2 > Z4. The spread is small, but it is systematic: **means favour Z2, geometric/median statistics favour Z4 or Z6**. This matters because PERM spans 13.5 orders of magnitude (min 0.00000000004002 to max 347.2514 mD across the interval; Z2's own min is 0.00000007184 at 608.838 m), so the choice of central tendency is not cosmetic — it changes the winner.

## Point 4 — What controls the ranking: shale volume, porosity, or hole condition?

**Porosity controls more than clay; hole condition is a complication.**

- **Shale volume contrast is weak.** The higher-PERM halves (Z2, Z4; 39.93 m) carry a thickness-weighted mean VCL of **0.8064 v/v** versus **0.8563 v/v** for the lower-PERM halves (Z3, Z5; 209.5 m) — a 0.05 v/v separation inside a 0.60–0.95 band. VCL's contribution to the PERM separation is therefore minor relative to the PHIE separation. All zones sit at SHALE_DOMINATED by the workflow's own threshold (>0.50 VCL).
- **Porosity carries the larger separation.** The same contrast gives PHIE **0.1114 v/v** (Z2+Z4) versus **0.07845 v/v** (Z3+Z5) — roughly a 1.4× difference, consistent with the RQI/PHI_Z construction (RQI = 0.0314·sqrt(PERM/PHIE), FZI = RQI/PHI_Z; the PORO_QUALITY_INDEX also tracks Z2/Z4 above Z3/Z5).
- **Hole condition cuts both ways.** BAD_HOLE > 0.5 covers 20.7% of Z2, 8.1% of Z4, and 57.4% of Z6. Z2 and Z6 are both partly bad-hole, so some of Z2's porosity/permeability advantage — and much of Z6's apparent FZI/RQI median strength — may rest on density-correction artefacts where caliper or DRHO excursions hit. The result shows PERM values exist on 100% of the bad-hole thickness (A and B overlap everywhere within zones), but **no per-depth correlation between BAD_HOLE and PERM is computed**, so hole condition cannot be quantitatively separated from real porosity. The multi-mineral lithology solver is also non-unique on its own (92 samples of 1860 have no lithology-log label), though mineral volumes were left solved at those same 92.

## Point 5 — Do the data weaknesses undermine the top zone's rank?

**Yes, partially — the Z2 lead is tail-sensitive and the PERM transform is uncalibrated.**

- **PERM basis:** Coates-Denoo empirical transform, k = 10000·PHIE⁴·((1−SWIRR)/SWIRR)², with SWIRR = 0.2717 taken as one well-wide low percentile (P5 of SW where resflag and RESD > 5, n=549), **clipped at 10000 mD**. There are **no core models at all** (same_well_core_models 0, peer_core_models 0, peer_wells_considered 0), so the permeability step rests entirely on the empirical transform of PHIE and SW.
- **Fit quality:** Z2's log10(PERM) on PHIE fit is the loosest of the front zones — slope 27.69, R² 0.5772, correlation 0.7597 (n=140, PERM 0.00000007184–205.2173, PHIE 0–0.2312) — versus Z4's R² 0.9587 / correlation 0.9791. A less-well-defined PERM-vs-PHIE relation means Z2's high mean PERM (inflated by its p90 of 117.456 mD and max 205.2173 mD, both in the 625 m–626 m transition area) rests on few tail samples, not on a tight joint.
- **Robustness check:** on the geometric mean — the statistic least sensitive to the 13.5-order-of-magnitude spread — **Z4 wins PERM** (9.2675 vs Z2's 3.8524 mD), and on FZI and RQI **medians Z6 wins** over Z2. So the uncalibrated transform does not change which zone is "best" under a mean view (Z2), but it does change who is best under tail-resistant statistics (Z4, and Z6 on FZI/RQI).
- **NET_PAY context (from earlier in this session):** the unzoned 33 samples (outside zone minima of ≥2 m) show mean PERM 278.3063 mD, mean PHIE 0.2114 v/v and a 15.2% NET_PAY fraction, but the workflow flags these as "not a zone" — thin runs of SHALY, SHALE-PRONE and CLEAN labels. The zoned Z2–Z6 share 0% NET_PAY under the cutoffs (VCL < 0.3, PHIE > 0.08, SW < 0.70), consistent with the earlier finding that the Mount Messenger in tuhua-7 is shale-prone and water-saturated.
