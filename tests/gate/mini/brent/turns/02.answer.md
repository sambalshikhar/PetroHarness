# Answer

**Best reservoir quality.** By the zone-mean rock-quality indicators, **Z19 (SAND_GROUP_12, 2951.6616–2955.0144 m)** leads: RQI mean 2.4187 µm and FZI mean 9.2246 µm are the highest of the 20 zones, and its 95.7% of samples carry the EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR class. The EXCELLENT_PERMEABILITY class in Z19 rests on one thick run, 2952.72852–2954.55737 m (~1.83 m of the 3.35 m zone) where PERM_CC exceeds 1000 mD. However, Z19 is uniformly water-flushed — SW = 1 across all 23 samples — carries 0 m net pay, and has no core plugs (KH_CORE n = 0 of 23). The zones that pair top reservoir quality with pay are **Z7 (2877.5955–2890.8542 m)** and **Z5 (2870.2803–2875.157 m)**: both EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR class with 97.7% and 87.9% of samples in that class respectively, low clay (VCL 0.0268 and 0.0557), low SW (0.2265 and 0.314), and net pay over 100% (Z7) and 90.9% (Z5) of their gross.

**Log permeability vs core.** Across the 141 zoned samples that pair Coates–Denoo PERM with KH_CORE, PERM sits systematically lower than the plug permeability: pooled means PERM 69.2312 mD vs KH_CORE 606.275 mD (difference −537.0439 mD, sd 1005.7811 mD), with PERM above KH_CORE in only 12.8% of paired samples and below in 87.2%. At zone level, PERM exceeds KH_CORE only in Z1 (37.42 vs 0.4786 mD, n = 7) and Z9 (16.45 vs 17.45 mD, n = 7); in every zone with more than a handful of plugs it falls well short — Z5: PERM 220.89 vs KH_CORE 1894.96 mD (n = 10); Z7: PERM 190.27 vs KH_CORE 1377.89 mD (n = 34). The gap is partly a calibration-scale problem rather than a physical one: the Coates–Denoo log is built on the uncorrected PHIE with an estimated irreducible water of 0.0638 (BVWirr from n = 216 samples), while KH_CORE measures actual packed-plug flow at in-situ conditions.

**Z19 vs pay.** The workflow's own quality screen (EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR where PHIE_CC > 0.15, permeability > 10 mD, VCL < 0.25) grades Z19 identically to Z4, Z5, Z7, Z13 and Z19 (35.6% of zoned thickness EXCELLENT; 44.9% of zone samples EXCELLENT). But reservoir quality and pay separate at the bottom of the zone table: Z19 sits at SW ~1 with no pay; Z5 and Z7 hold 90.9% and 100% of net pay with SW 0.314 and 0.2265. Geophysical hydrocarbon indicators report NO_INDICATION over 100% of the interval from the earlier session; PRIME_OIL_TARGET covers 174 samples / 26.51 m and NET_PAY 185 samples / 28.5 m, none in Z19.

## Point 4 — Log permeability vs core plug permeability

**Calibration framework.** The same-well core model fitted n = 198 plugs against PHIC/KH_CORE: PHIT_CC = 1.136·PHIT − 0.039 with r² = 0.55 (n = 200 plugs). # Limitations

- **Model outputs behind every curve:** PHIT, PHIE, VCL, SW, SXO, PERM, PERM_CC, RQI, FZI, MHI, INVASION_RATIO are workflow model outputs. The primary data are measurements (RHOB, NPHI, CALI, BS, DTC, DTE, SP, resistivity curves, PHIC, KH_CORE, KV_CORE).
- **Fallback inputs:** FZI/RQI substitute PERM for PERM_CC at 144 of 576 samples; PHIE_CC floors at 0 in 20% of zoned samples; PERM floors at 0 in 10%; SW reads 1 in 36% of zoned samples and SXO reads 1 in 61% — defaults or limits that flatten water saturation in the flanks of Z1–Z2, Z10–Z12, Z13–Z15, Z19.
- **Calibration strength:** PHIT_CC r² = 0.55 (n = 200) is a weak porosity fit; PERM_CC is valid only for VCL ≤ 0.30 samples (437 of 768), so 190 zoned samples — mostly shales — have no calibrated permeability and their FZI/RQI come from raw Coates–Denoo PERM.
- **Missing measurements:** no DTS in the LAS input; RXO (Resistivity – Flushed Zone) is the absent resistivity curve. The shallow-to-deep ratio in this interval is built from RSHA over RDEP at 768 samples — no medium resistivity stands in for a shallow one here — with RSHA read at 22079 samples well-wide and RMED at 92 samples well-wide as fallback where shallow curves were unavailable or non-finite. SXO is assembled from shallow resistivity at 494 samples in this interval and falls back to SW^(1/5) at 92 samples well-wide (fifth_root_quicklook=92). What is actually missing is RXO itself and DTS.

# What the well lacks

- Gas samples or log-bearing fluid confirmations; a depth tie between core intercepts and log runs; MHI confirmation from geophysics (X-only invasion at 494 samples).
