# Reservoir quality and permeability vs core — Brent Group, 30/6-5 (2841.0193–2957.9102 m)

## Direct answer

**Best reservoir quality: Z7 SAND_GROUP_3, 2877.5955–2890.8542 m.** It is the only zone that combines all four pay ingredients together: mean VCL 0.02675 v/v (essentially clean), mean SW 0.2265 v/v (lowest among pay zones), mean PHIE_CC 0.2186 v/v / PHIE 0.2256 v/v (highest among pay zones), and NET_PAY on 100% of its samples (88/88, 13.4111 m of pay, pay SW 0.22, pay PHIE 0.2256). By the workflow's own classifier it sits as EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR on 97.7% of its samples (GOOD on 2.3%) — 44.9% of the zoned thickness carries that class.

**On permeability alone, Z5 CROSSBEDDED SAND_GROUP_6 (2870.2803–2875.157 m) leads**, and Z19 SAND_GROUP_12 (2951.6616–2955.0144 m) tops the absolute curve: Z5 mean PERM 220.9 mD / mean PERM_CC 864.6 mD with EXCELLENT_PERMEABILITY over 42.4% of samples and 90.9% pay; Z19 mean PERM_CC 1627.6 mD, FZI 9.22 µm, RQI 2.42 µm, EXCELLENT_PERMEABILITY over 56.5% of samples. But Z19 carries SW = 1.0 across the whole zone and zero NET_PAY, and Z5's mean SW is 0.314 v/v, so the pay-weighted call stays with Z7.

**Log vs core: core-calibrated permeability runs systematically above the log curve, and PHIE_CC provides the core-calibrated porosity.** In the zones pooled, PERM (Coates-Denoo, uncalibrated) mean 70.6 mD (n 472 of 567) vs PERM_CC mean 342.5 mD (n 363 of 567). Over the 361 paired rows, PERM_CC exceeds PERM in 98.1% of samples (mean difference PERM − PERM_CC = −253.5 mD; mean/sd −0.46, sd 557.1 mD). Zone by zone the gap is about 2.2× in Z7 (190.3 vs 416.0 mD) and 3.9× in Z5 (220.9 vs 864.6 mD). Against KH_CORE the gap is much wider: PERM sits below KH_CORE in 87.2% of the 141 paired rows (mean PERM 69.2 mD vs mean KH_CORE 606.3 mD), and Z7's KH_CORE mean is 1377.9 mD.

**PERM_CC credibility: indicated, not confirmed.** PHIE_CC and PERM_CC exist in this interval and are core-calibrated outputs of a same-well model fitted on this well's core (n=198 from PHIC and KH_CORE). The porosity transform that drives it — PHIT_CC = 1.136·PHIT − 0.039, n=200, r²=0.55 — is printed. But PERM_CC has values at only 437 of 768 samples, so 331 of the interval's 768 samples lack it (768 − 437); it is absent entirely over Z2, Z3, Z10, Z11, Z12 and Z17. The coal/silt bad-hole samples (65 of 768 flagged, 9.9 m) fall outside the core-corrected sections, and RQI/FZI silently fell back to the uncalibrated Coates-Denoo PERM on 144 of 576 rock-quality samples where PERM_CC is missing.

## 2. Log permeability vs core

**Finding:** The core-calibrated curve is higher than the log curve almost everywhere. Pooled zones: PERM 70.6 mD mean vs PERM_CC 342.5 mD mean (≈4.9× on the pooled mean of PERM_CC 342.5 / PERM 70.6); paired rows: PERM_CC > PERM in 98.1%, mean gap −253.5 mD. Against KH_CORE (core plug horizontal permeability, the actual core measurement) the log is much lower: PERM < KH_CORE in 87.2% of the 141 paired rows.

**Numbers and intervals.**
- Zones pooled: PERM mean 70.6183 mD, n 472 of 567; PERM_CC mean 342.4819 mD, n 363 of 567 (core-calibrated, same-well model); PERM_CC spans 6.3 orders of magnitude, percentiles read on log10, geometric mean added.
- The result (361 paired rows): mean PERM 90.837, mean PERM_CC 344.3793, difference −253.5423, sd 557.1437, mean/sd −0.4551.
- detail: Z7 PERM median 199.8 / geo 160.1 vs PERM_CC median 354.5 / geo 316.6 (core-calibrated); Z5 PERM median 211.1 vs PERM_CC median 913.2 / geo 307.6; Z4 PERM_CC median 279.8 / geo 194.9; Z19 PERM_CC median 1332.6 / geo 802.3; pooled (Z4–Z5, Z7, Z19): PERM median 169.8, PERM_CC median 408.0, geo 334.9.
- The result vs KH_CORE (n 141): mean PERM 69.2312 vs mean KH_CORE 606.275, difference −537.0439, sd 1005.7811; PERM < KH_CORE in 87.2%.
- Zone table KH_CORE: Z7 mean 1377.8912 mD (n 7 core samples), Z5 mean 1894.96 mD, Z1 mean 0.4786 mD, Z18 mean 0.04 mD — KH_CORE itself spans 5.5 orders of magnitude (0.06–5826 mD), so the pooled mean KH_CORE (535.9304 mD) pools unlike samples and must not be read as a representative zone value.

**Limits.** The factor is not constant: on paired-sample medians PERM_CC/PERM is ~1.8× (354.5/199.8 in Z7) while zone means imply ~2.2× in Z7 and ~3.9× in Z5; pooled means imply ~4.9× because PERM_CC's geometric mean (334.9 mD) sits well above its dispersion. The 10% of samples where PERM reads 0 (a limit/default) and the absence of PERM_CC in Z2, Z3, Z10, Z11, Z12 and Z17 mean the comparison covers only a subset of the interval.

## 3. Credibility of the PERM_CC core transform

**Finding:** PHIE_CC and PERM_CC are core-calibrated model outputs fitted on this well's own core (same-well core model, n=198 from PHIC and KH_CORE). They are indicated as a credible upward-shift on clean sand, but unproven as a calibrated permeability field: the transform exists, its porosity anchor is r²=0.55, yet no permeability fit statistics are printed, coverage is partial (437 of 768 samples, leaving 331 without PERM_CC), the bad-hole and coal samples sit outside it, and much of the FZI/RQI reading leans on the uncalibrated fallback.

**Evidence and limits.**
- The data set includes PHIE_CC (core-calibrated effective porosity, v/v) with values at 567 of 567 zoned samples, mean 0.1254 v/v; PERM_CC (core-calibrated permeability, mD) with values at 437 of 768 samples over 2842.3911–2956.0813 m.
- The same-well core model record shows `same_well_core_models 1`, model `30/6-5 / all_depths` (n=198, core_phi=PHIC, core_perm=KH_CORE); porosity transform PHIT_CC = 1.136·PHIT − 0.039 (n=200, r²=0.55); `core_min_points 3`, `core_porosity_min_r_squared 0.5`; PERM_CC written only where VCL ≤ 0.30 and where the clay volume its core plugs span (P95 0.85); `perm_max_clip_md 10000`.
- Coverage: PERM_CC at 437 of 768 samples; absent in Z2, Z3, Z10, Z11, Z12 and Z17. BAD_HOLE flagged at 65 of 768 samples (9.9 m) with density, neutron, PHIE and SW called unreliable. RESFLAG set at 413 of 768 samples.
- compares PHIC vs PHIE, PHIC vs KH_CORE, PHIC vs PERM_CC by lag cross-correlation, but the printed summary lists the headings without the visible coefficients, so the correlation strengths are referenced but not readable in this listing.
- Consequences: the permeability analyzer prefers PERM_CC (`PERM_CC -> PERM`), and on Z7/Z5 it consistently raises permeability above the Coates-Denoo log. But because 331 of 768 samples lack PERM_CC and Z17 is entirely unmodelled, the flow-unit shares are partly an artifact of the fallback, and the offset pattern that would confirm transform shape is only partially visible in the result.

**Logs the well lacks:** DTS (needed for the geophysical hydrocarbon screen, not computable here — NO_INDICATION covers uncomputable samples) and RXO (so SXO is the shallow-resistivity proxy, equal to 1 where SW is 1 on 274 samples), which together limit how far saturation-based confidence extends into Z19's SW=1 and the bad-hole stretch.

## Limitations that shape the answer

- **Model outputs (not measurements):** PERM is Coates-Denoo empirical output with a fallback chain that clips gas/crossover/weighted-ND into defaults (PERM=0 in 10% of zoned samples); PERM_CC, RQI, FZI, VCL, SW/SXO, MHI are all workflow model outputs.
- **Fallbacks:** FZI/RQI used PERM wherever PERM_CC is missing (144 of 576 samples) — a stated fallback input, not a calibrated core value; SW/SXO used shallow-resistivity proxies because RXO is absent.
- **Hold/limits:** PERM_CC clipped at 10000 mD; PHIE_CC and PHI_SEC floor at 0 (20% and 94% of zoned samples respectively); MHI maxes at 1 on 60% of samples (a limit/default); SXO=1 where SW=1 on 274 samples.
- **Not computable from this well:** the geophysical hydrocarbon screen needs DTS, which is not in the input; no lag-correlation coefficients for PHIC vs PERM_CC are visible in the summary listing.
