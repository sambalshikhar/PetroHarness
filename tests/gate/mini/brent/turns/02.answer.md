# Best reservoir quality zone and log-vs-core permeability agreement in Brent Group, 30_6-5 (2841.02–2957.91 m)

## The answer in one place

**Best reservoir quality (RQI/FZI, core-calibrated permeability).** Z19 (SAND, 2951.66–2955.01 m) is the best on RQI/FZI and PERM_CC: mean FZI 9.22 µm, RQI 2.42 µm, PERM_CC 1627.6 mD (geometric mean 802.3 mD), highest of all 20 zones, classified EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR by 95.7% of its samples. **But Z19 is water-bearing throughout** — SW = 1.0 v/v on all 23 of its samples — so it is reservoir-quality rock, not pay-ready rock. The pay-ready (SW < 0.7) excellent-reservoir zones are **Z5** (CROSSBEDDED SAND, 2870.28–2875.16 m: SW 0.314, VCL 0.056, RQI 1.67, FZI 5.53, PERM_CC 864.6 mD; 87.9% of samples EXCELLENT) and **Z7** (SAND, 2877.60–2890.85 m: SW 0.227, VCL 0.027, RQI 1.27, FZI 4.43, PERM_CC 416.0 mD; 97.7% EXCELLENT). Among zones that are both excellent and pay-ready, **Z5 has the highest RQI/FZI/PERM_CC**; Z7 is cleaner (lowest VCL and SW) and much thicker (13.26 m vs 4.88 m) but lower RQI. Z4 (CROSSBEDDED SAND, 2865.86–2869.37 m; SW 0.411, 75% EXCELLENT) is the third pay-ready excellent zone.

**Log (Coates-Denoo PERM) vs core (KH_CORE).** The agreement is weak-to-moderate overall and zone-dependent. Over 149 overlapping core samples, log10(PERM) vs log10(KH_CORE) gives R² 0.513, slope 0.727, bias −0.826, RMSE 1.275 — the log reads compressed and low relative to the plugs, so it is **not a calibrated core tracer**. Fits are strong only in thin plug sets (Z6 R² 0.936, Z16 R² 0.9999, Z10 R² 0.977 but bias −2.23) and weak-to-negligible in the main sands (Z1 0.011, Z7 0.052, Z13 0.046, Z15 0.093). On the other side, the core-calibrated PERM_CC consistently exceeds the uncalibrated PERM (over 361 zone-level pairs, Spearman 0.934; zone means 0.974; PERM_CC higher in all 14 zones with both curves, mean difference −253.5 mD). The transform rests on a same-well porosity model with only r² 0.55 (n 200), and Z19's high PERM_CC has **no core plugs behind it** (0 of 23 samples).

## Point 2 — How well does log permeability agree with core plug permeability

### Evidence

**Full-zone fit (log10 space).** Zones pooled and whole interval: n 149 plugs, R² 0.513, bias −0.826 (log − core), RMSE 1.275; fitted line log = 0.7274·core − 0.3297. Slope 0.727 is well below 1 and the bias is ~0.8 log10 (log PERM under-reads KH_CORE by roughly 70% in log units), so the log is not a calibrated core tracer.

**Zone-by-zone fit.** Strong only where plug counts are small: Z6 n 3, R² 0.9364, RMSE 1.03; Z16 n 3, R² 0.9999, RMSE 0.51; Z10 n 4, R² 0.9767 but bias −2.2277, RMSE 2.27. Weak to negligible in the sand bodies: Z1 n 7, R² 0.0114; Z7 n 34, R² 0.0524; Z13 n 11, R² 0.0461; Z15 n 35, R² 0.0927.

**Log vs core-calibrated.** Over 361 zone-level pairs with both PERM and PERM_CC, PERM_CC is higher than PERM in all 14 zones (PERM > PERM_CC in 1.9%, < in 98.1%); zone-mean Spearman 0.9736; mean difference −253.5 mD with sd 557.1 (mean/sd −0.455). Over the whole 768-sample interval (n 435 pairs): mean PERM 84.9 mD vs PERM_CC 375.3 mD, Spearman 0.919. Z19 shows the largest gap: mean PERM 103.1 vs PERM_CC 1627.6 (difference −1524.5 mD, n 23/23), with PERM max 310.7 and PERM_CC max 5261.2.

**Porosity arm behind the transform.** The same-well core model uses n=198 for permeability and n=200 for the porosity correction; core porosity reads at Z5 show PHIC mean 0.2359 against PHIE_CC-at-core-depths mean 0.2006 (close), and at Z7 PHIC 0.2279 vs PHIE_CC 0.2183 (close). The porosity arm carries **r² 0.55** (record, core_correction step), so the whole PERM_CC transform inherits moderate uncertainty. Depth alignment rests on a single pooled fit plus one correlation at zero lag; no lag analysis was run.

### Reading

The direction is consistent — PERM_CC tracks above PERM — and the rank correlation is high (0.93–0.97). But in value space the log is compressed and biased low against KH_CORE (R² 0.513, slope 0.73, bias −0.83 log10), and the agreement is not uniform: it is decent in thin, homogeneous plug sets (Z6, Z16) and poor in the thicker sands (Z7, Z13, Z15). KH_CORE spans more than two orders of magnitude in Z5 (p10 13.34, p90 5461 mD), so any mean comparison is wide-tailed; 169 overlapping pairs span that range. A single correlation at zero lag is the only depth-alignment evidence. Because the porosity arm is only r² 0.55, the PERM_CC curve — and hence RQI/FZI, which consume it — is best read as a same-well transform indicator, not a plug-verified calibration.

## Limitations on this answer

- **Model outputs and fallbacks.** PERM is the Coates-Denoo empirical output (`10000·PHIE⁴·((1−SWIRR)/SWIRR)²`, clipped at 10 000 mD); PERM_CC is the same-well core transform (only 437 of 768 samples have it; 331 of 768 lack it). FZI/RQI use PERM_CC where present and fall back to PERM at 144 of 576 samples, so parts of the index are built on the uncalibrated log.
- **Core coverage is sparse.** KH_CORE exists on only 197 of 768 interval samples (37 outside the zones); Z19, Z2, Z3, Z4, Z17 and Z20 have zero or one plugs, so their RQI/FZI/PERM_CC grades rest entirely on the fitted transform.
- **Transform uncertainty.** The same-well core model has n=198/200 with a porosity arm at r² 0.55; no peer-well models were fitted.
- **Depth alignment.** One pooled zero-lag correlation (R² 0.513) plus per-zone regressions; no lag analysis for PHIC vs log porosity was run.
- **Missing logs.** DTS and RXO are not in the input (the geophysical hydrocarbon indicator could not compute: NO_INDICATION on 100% of samples), and gas confirmation relies on the weak WEAK_GAS_EFFECT signatue from the earlier session (1.64 m in Z1/Z5/Z7, crossover flag at 0 samples). SW/SXO are Archie+Simandoux model outputs; fluid presence is log-indicated, not proven.
