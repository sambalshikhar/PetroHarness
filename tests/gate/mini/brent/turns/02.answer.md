# Reservoir quality and log vs core permeability — well 30_6-5, Brent Group (2841.0193–2957.9102 m)

Building on the earlier session's porosity and saturation read of the Brent, the points below add the quality ranking (RQI/FZI, core-calibrated porosity/permeability), the log-permeability-versus-core comparison, and the pay coincidence.

## Point 1 — Which zone has the best reservoir quality, and does log permeability compare with core permeability?

**Z19 (SAND_GROUP_12, 2951.6616–2955.0144 m, 23 samples) holds the best reservoir quality.** By the zone means it leads every zone on every core-calibrated quality curve:

- RQI mean **2.4187 µm** (median 2.5383, geometric mean 2.0168) — highest of all 20 zones
- FZI mean **9.2246 µm** (geometric mean 8.325) — highest of all zones
- PERM_CC mean **1627.6136 mD** (median 1332.5705, geometric mean 802.347) — highest; values span 23.2 to 5261.2 mD
- PHIE_CC mean **0.1967**, PHIT mean **0.2462** — highest
- VCL mean **0.1928** — within the EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR threshold of < 0.25 v/v

On the workflow's own reservoir-quality screen (EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR = effective porosity > 0.15 v/v, permeability > 10 mD, clay volume < 0.25 v/v, applied with PERM_CC priority): Z19 classifies as EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR on **95.7% of its samples**; 37.4 m (44.9% of zoned thickness) is EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR across Z4–Z5, Z7, Z13 and Z19. Z19 is graded GHE_7 by mean FZI, with 43.5% of its samples in GHE_7, 30.4% in GHE_8, 21.7% in GHE_6 — the only zone with systematic FZI ≥ 6 µm.

**Log permeability versus core permeability: the uncalibrated Coates-Denoo PERM runs below PERM_CC everywhere.** Over the 361 pooled samples with both curves (14 zones), PERM < PERM_CC in **98.1%** of samples (PERM above in 1.9%, equal in 0.0%); mean difference **−253.5 mD** against SD 557.1 mD, rank correlation 0.9343 (Spearman) — the ranking is preserved even though the levels are not. The gap is largest in Z19 (−1524.5 mD mean: PERM 103.1 vs PERM_CC 1627.6), next Z5 (−636.8), then Z7 (−225.7). In zones without PERM_CC (Z2, Z3, Z10–Z12, Z17), FZI/RQI fall back to uncalibrated PERM, so the top of the quality ranking is carried by the core-calibrated curve (the result notes the fallback at 144 of 576 samples).

## Point 3 — How PERM (Coates-Denoo, uncalibrated) compares with PERM_CC across zones and pooled

The whole-well core transform (198 plugs: PHIC, KH_CORE) was fitted once and applied only where VCL ≤ 0.30 and the clay span of the plugs holds; PERM_CC is computed on 437 of 768 samples (perimeter step record). The comparison:

- **Pooled (361 samples with both curves, over zones):** PERM mean 90.84 mD vs PERM_CC mean 344.38 mD; difference mean −253.54 mD (mean/sd −0.455); PERM < PERM_CC in 98.1% of samples. Whole interval (435 samples): mean −290.4 mD.
- **Zone by zone (14 zones with overlapping values):** PERM < PERM_CC in 100% of samples in every zone; rank correlation runs from 0.7194 (Z4) to 0.9729 (Z15) across the 14 zones (Spearman). The downward bias scales with PERM_CC magnitude: mean/sd of the difference is about −1.0 to −1.2 in the high-permeability zones (Z19 −1.1138, Z5 −1.0173, Z7 −1.0206, Z4 −1.1915), i.e. the gap is proportional, not a constant offset.
- **Calibration check:** across the 149 plugs in the whole interval / pooled zones, R² = 0.513, bias −0.8257 log units, RMSE 1.275 log units, fitted line log = 0.7274·core − 0.3297. Zone R²s are weak on small n (Z1 0.01139, Z13 0.04606, Z7 0.05242; Z9 0.6167, Z10 0.9767, Z16 0.9999). The log-permeability curve therefore does not track core KH_CORE tightly: a systematic low bias with scatter of order 1 log unit. The transform's scatter and bias weaken any precision reading of the PERM–PERM_CC gap, especially in Z19 where the pooled gap is −1524.5 mD but the RMSE in log10 is ~1.3.

## Point 4 — Does the best-quality zone coincide with pay, or is it water-filled?

**The best-quality zone is water-filled and carries no pay; the pay-bearing excellent reservoirs are Z5 and Z7.**

- **Z19: SW mean 1.0** (all 23 samples at 1), SXO 1, BVW mean 0.1967 — indicated water saturation from the saturation step (Archie on clean samples, Simandoux routed for shaly samples at salinity 30,489 ppm). NET_PAY share in Z19 is **0%**. No core-saturation tie breaks the SW = 1 reading.
- **Z5: SW mean 0.314**, NET_PAY **90.9%**, PRIME_OIL_TARGET within it (the earlier session's 26.51 m PRIME_OIL_TARGET and 62.92 m NET_RES span the pay sands including Z5/Z7). Z5's pooled SW over the 144 pay-flagged zone samples is 0.3701, dragged by non-pay samples inside that pool; the per-zone SW is 0.314.
- **Z7: SW mean 0.2265**, NET_PAY **100%** — the most completely pay-bearing zone.

So: Z19 is the best reservoir *quality* rock (highest RQI/FZI/PERM_CC, EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR on 95.7% of samples) but it is logged as fully water-saturated with no net pay — high-quality rock that is water-filled, not hydrocarbon-bearing. The hydrocarbon-bearing pay concentrates in Z5 and Z7, which rank second and third in the quality ordering.

Reading notes and limits on the evidence:

- **Curves used:** all of PERM, PERM_CC, PHIE, PHIE_CC, FZI, RQI, VCL, SW, SXO, BVW and NET_PAY are model outputs of the workflow steps (porosity, core_correction, permeability, saturation, rock_quality, net_pay) over a vendor lithology log; they are not measurements. PHI_Z/RQI/FZI use PERM_CC first and fall back to uncalibrated PERM where PERM_CC is absent (144 of 576 zone samples), so the FZI/RQI ranking leans on the core transform.
- **Z19's RQI/FZI are carried by PERM_CC:** with only 23 samples in Z19 and PERM_CC present on all of them, the zone mean is sensitive to the core transform's bias and scatter (RMSE 1.275 log10; 6.3 orders of magnitude span across the zones).
- **Saturation is a model indication, not a core tie:** SW = 1 in 53% of zoned samples; Z19's SW = 1 rests on Archie on clean rock and the Simandoux routing at 30,489 ppm salinity. MHI is computable on 635 of 768 samples (MHI 0.3168–1.0), but with SXO from shallow resistivity at only 358 samples and "1 where SW is 1 too" at 410 samples, pay is a log-flag, not a measured fluid.
- **Z5 SW pool warning:** the 0.3701 mean over the 144 pay-flagged zone samples includes non-pay samples; the zone line itself gives SW mean 0.314.
- **Unzoned pay:** 15 of the 185 NET_PAY = 1 samples (2.283 m) sit outside any zone label, so zone-level pay shares are partial.
- **Data gaps:** PERM absent in 10% of zoned samples (limit/default 0), PERM_CC absent in Z2, Z3, Z10, Z11, Z12, Z17; FZI/RQI absent entirely in Z17. The 201 unzoned samples carry a large part of the pooled PERM/PERM_CC variation (PERM_CC mean 525.9 mD, n=74 there vs 342.5 pooled over zones).

## Limitations

- **Model outputs throughout:** PHIE/CC, PERM, PERM_CC, RQI, FZI, SW, SXO, BVW, VCL and all pay flags are model outputs. DTS and RXO are not in the input, so the geophysical hydrocarbon indication reads NO_INDICATION only because it is uncomputable — not because the interval is ruled out. SXO over the shallow-resistivity route is sparse (358 samples), which caps confidence in invasion-based pay.
- **Core-calibrated permeability is a whole-well transform** fitted to this well's 198 plugs (PHIC, KH_CORE), applied only where VCL ≤ 0.30 and within the plugs' clay span; positive PERM_CC values span 6.3 orders of magnitude, and the log-permeability-to-core scatter (R² 0.513, RMSE 1.275 log units, systematic −0.826 log-unit bias) limits precision on the PERM vs PERM_CC gap — particularly in Z19, where the gap is 6 orders of magnitude and the zone has only 23 samples.
- **Threshold screens rest on logs alone:** EXCELLENT_RESERVOIR uses its recorded criteria (effective porosity > 0.15 v/v, permeability > 10 mD, clay < 0.25 v/v) with PERM_CC priority; Z19 qualifies on 95.7% of samples, but this is a class assignment from the workflow, not core-verified porosity or saturation.
- **No production-relevant fluid confirmation:** pay (NET_PAY 185 samples, 28.16 m) and SW are log-indicated; PRIME_OIL_TARGET flags 174 samples (26.51 m) within them. Nothing in the logs ties a fluid to the reservoirs.
